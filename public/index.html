The problem

Why does my tailbone hurt when I sit?

The coccyx, or tailbone, is the small bone at the very bottom of your spine. Every time you sit, and especially when you lean back, part of your weight rests on it. If it has been bruised, strained or pushed out of position by a fall, childbirth or long hours of sitting, that pressure turns into pain. Doctors call this coccydynia.

Does this sound familiar?

  • Pain right at the base of the spine
  • Worse the longer you sit, or when you lean back
  • A sharp jolt when you get up from a chair
  • Pain during bowel movements or intercourse

The good news: it is very treatable, and surgery is rarely needed.

How we fix it

Four steps from pain to comfortable sitting

Non-surgical, hands-on care, explained before anything is done. This is what happens at the clinic.

  1. Listen & assess

    We listen to how the pain started, what makes it worse, and how it affects your day, followed by a careful physical examination.

  2. Pinpoint the cause

    We work out whether the coccyx is mobile, stiff or displaced, and which surrounding tissues are involved.

  3. Hands-on treatment

    Internal Manipulation: a short, discreet procedure done with care and consent. Many patients feel a difference after the first session.

  4. Recover & prevent

    Posture coaching, targeted exercises and seating advice, plus a follow-up check, so the pain stays away.
