Understanding coccydynia

A small bone that can take over your whole day.

The coccyx is the last few small vertebrae at the base of your spine. It carries part of your weight every time you sit and anchors several muscles, tendons and ligaments of the pelvic floor. When it's injured, inflamed or out of position, the pain is called coccydynia.

It often follows a fall onto the backside, childbirth, long hours on hard seats, or repetitive strain like cycling or rowing. Sometimes there's no clear cause. Either way, it is treatable, and it rarely needs surgery.

How we treat it

A clear, gentle path back to comfortable sitting.

Our core technique is Internal Manipulation (IM): a precise, hands-on, non-surgical method that releases the tight muscles and ligaments around the coccyx and restores how it moves. It's combined with a plan built around your own body and lifestyle.

  1. 01

    Listen & assess

    A detailed history and physical examination. Bring any X-ray or MRI reports you already have.

  2. 02

    Pinpoint the cause

    We work out whether the coccyx is mobile, stiff or displaced, and which surrounding tissues are involved.

  3. 03

    Internal Manipulation

    A short, discreet procedure done with care and consent. Many patients feel a difference after the first session.

  4. 04

    Recover & prevent

    Posture coaching, targeted exercises and seating advice, including our TBT cushion, so the pain stays away.

While you wait for your visit

Five small changes that ease tailbone pain.

1

Lean slightly forward

Shifting your weight onto your thighs takes load off the coccyx.

2

Use a cut-out cushion

A wedge or U-shaped cushion leaves the tailbone hanging free.

3

Stand up every 30 min

Short breaks stop pressure and stiffness from building up.

4

Avoid very soft sofas

Sinking in rolls you back onto the coccyx. Firm, supportive seats are better.

5

Keep things moving

Fibre and water help prevent straining, which often aggravates the pain.

    FAQ

    Questions patients often ask.

    What is coccydynia?

    It's the medical name for pain in and around the coccyx (tailbone). It usually gets worse when sitting, especially when leaning back, and when standing up after sitting.

    What is Internal Manipulation, and does it hurt?

    It's a hands-on, non-surgical technique that frees up the tight ligaments and muscles attached to the coccyx and restores its alignment and movement. It is quick and done with full consent and privacy. Patients commonly report it's far less uncomfortable than they expected.

    How many sessions will I need?

    It depends on your diagnosis, how long you've had the pain and your general health. Some people need very few visits, and others need a short course. We reassess your progress at every visit.

    What should I bring to my appointment?

    Your medical and surgical history, any X-ray or MRI reports, and a list of current medications. Wear comfortable clothing.

    Do I need a referral from a doctor?

    No. You can book directly with us by phone, WhatsApp or the form above.

    Is a female clinician available?

    Yes. A female specialist trained by Dr Rajveer Singh is available. Just mention it when you book.

    I live outside India. Can you help?

    Yes. Many of our patients travel from abroad, including from the Middle East. Start with an online consultation and we'll help you plan the visit.

    How do I cancel or reschedule?

    Open My appointments, enter your booking reference and phone number, and pick a new time or cancel. You can also call or WhatsApp +91 98866 88323.
