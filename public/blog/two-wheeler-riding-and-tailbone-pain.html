Two-wheelers, potholes and tailbone pain: what the research shows

A 2025 study found tailbone pain in a third of regular motorbike riders, and vibration was the strongest risk factor. What that means for daily commuters in Delhi, and how to protect your coccyx.

By Tailbone Treatment Clinic · 17 October 2026 · 5 min read

Share on WhatsApp

For millions of people in Indian cities, the daily commute means a scooter or motorbike: speed breakers, potholes and long rides in traffic. Each bump travels up through a narrow seat to the base of your spine. Research from our region suggests that this matters for the tailbone.

Key points

  • A 2025 study of 190 male riders in Pakistan found tailbone pain in 33.2%.
  • Vibration exposure was the strongest risk factor: riders exposed to it were almost 6 times more likely to have pain.
  • Poor posture on the bike roughly tripled the odds.
  • Riders with weak core muscles (unable to hold a plank) all reported pain.
  • Long sitting hours alone, bike type and bike fit were not significantly linked to pain in this study.

About the evidence: this is one cross-sectional study of young men in Pakistan. It shows an association, not proof that riding causes coccydynia. We have not found a similar study in India yet, but road conditions and commuting habits are very similar.

Why vibration is hard on the coccyx

The coccyx is a small bone with several joints, held by ligaments and the pelvic-floor muscles. A single hard bump can bruise it. Thousands of smaller jolts every week may strain the ligaments and keep the area irritated, especially if you are already sitting at a desk for hours. The MSD Manual lists motorcycling and cycling among the activities that patients with coccydynia are advised to limit while they recover.

Protecting your tailbone on two wheels

  1. Slow down for speed breakers and potholes, and lift slightly off the seat for big bumps.
  2. Sit upright, slightly forward, with your weight on your sitting bones rather than slouched back.
  3. Check your seat: add a firm, shock-absorbing seat cover, and keep your tyres at the right pressure.
  4. Strengthen your core: simple plank and bridge exercises support the pelvis.
  5. Break up long rides, and combine this with regular breaks if you also sit all day.
  6. If it already hurts, take a break from riding, or ride pillion with a cut-out cushion, until you have been assessed.
