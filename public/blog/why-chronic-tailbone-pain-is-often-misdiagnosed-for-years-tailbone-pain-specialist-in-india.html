Chronic tailbone pain, also known as coccydynia, can significantly affect daily life. Many patients spend years seeking treatment for lower back pain, sciatica, piles, or hip problems before discovering that the actual source of their discomfort is the coccyx. According to Dr. Rajveer Singh, proper diagnosis is the first step toward effective non-surgical recovery.

Why Tailbone Pain is Commonly Misdiagnosed

The coccyx is located near several structures that can also cause pain. Because symptoms often overlap, many patients receive treatment for unrelated conditions while coccyx dysfunction remains undetected.

Tailbone Pain vs Lower Back Pain

While lower back pain usually affects the lumbar region, coccyx pain is often localized at the bottom of the spine and becomes worse while sitting or rising from a seated position.

The Importance of Sitting Pain

Pain that increases during prolonged sitting is one of the strongest indicators of coccyx dysfunction. Many patients report discomfort while driving, working at a desk, or sitting on hard surfaces.

Understanding Dynamic Coccyx Dysfunction

In some cases, the coccyx moves excessively or becomes restricted during sitting and standing movements. These functional problems may not appear on routine imaging studies.

How Physiotherapy Helps

Specialized rehabilitation focuses on:

  • Posture correction
  • Pelvic alignment
  • Muscle release techniques
  • Core strengthening
  • Sitting mechanics training
  • Functional movement restoration

Conclusion

Many cases of chronic tailbone pain remain undiagnosed for years because the symptoms mimic other conditions. A thorough functional assessment and personalized physiotherapy approach can help identify the real cause and provide long-term relief.

FAQs

1. What is chronic tailbone pain? Chronic tailbone pain is persistent pain around the coccyx that lasts for several weeks or months.

2. Why is tailbone pain often misdiagnosed? Its symptoms can resemble lower back pain, sciatica, pelvic pain, or hemorrhoids.

3. Can tailbone pain occur without injury? Yes. Poor posture, prolonged sitting, childbirth, and muscle imbalances can contribute to coccyx pain.

4. Can physiotherapy help chronic coccyx pain? Yes. Specialized physiotherapy can improve mobility, posture, and muscle function around the coccyx.

5. Does every patient with tailbone pain need surgery? No. Most patients respond well to conservative treatment and rehabilitation.

Please note: this article is general information, not a diagnosis or a substitute for a consultation. If you have any of the red-flag symptoms, see a doctor promptly.
