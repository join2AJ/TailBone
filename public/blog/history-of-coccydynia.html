A short history of tailbone pain: from 1859 to today

Tailbone pain was named "coccygodynia" in 1859. How doctors' understanding grew, from pelvic floor spasm to coccyx shapes and sitting X-rays, and what it means for treatment today.

By Tailbone Treatment Clinic · 17 October 2026 · 4 min read

Share on WhatsApp

Tailbone pain is not a new problem. It has been described for centuries, and named for more than 160 years. Knowing how ideas about it developed explains why treatment today looks at the bone, the joints and the muscles.

Key points

  • Accounts of pain at the tailbone go back to the 16th century.
  • In 1859, the Scottish obstetrician Sir James Young Simpson named it coccygodynia, from the Greek for coccyx and pain.
  • In 1950 and 1963, George Thiele linked many cases to spasm of the pelvic floor muscles, and treated them with massage of those muscles.
  • In 1983, Postacchini and Massobrio described the different shapes of the coccyx.
  • In 2000, Maigne and colleagues used X-rays taken sitting and standing to show how the coccyx moves, and linked body weight to the type of problem.
  • In 2014, a study found tailbone pain about three times more often in people with ankylosing spondylitis than in people with ordinary back pain.

What it means today

Each step added a piece of the picture:

  • The muscles matter. Thiele's work is why hands-on treatment of the pelvic floor is still part of care today.
  • The shape and movement of the coccyx matter. Postacchini and Maigne showed that the coccyx's shape and how it moves when you sit help explain the pain and guide treatment. Read more in coccyx shapes and movement.
  • Other conditions can be involved. Newer research links tailbone pain with inflammatory spine disease, pelvic floor problems and more. See conditions linked to tailbone pain.

That is why our treatment combines a careful history and examination, hands-on treatment of both the coccyx and the muscles, and advice on sitting. See coccydynia treatment, step by step.
