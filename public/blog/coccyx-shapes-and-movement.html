The shape and movement of your tailbone: why some hurt and others don't

Coccyges come in different shapes and move differently when you sit. Here is what the four classic coccyx types mean, and how too much or too little movement causes pain.

By Tailbone Treatment Clinic · 17 October 2026 · 6 min read

Your coccyx is not a fixed, useless stub. It is made of three to five small bones, usually partly fused, and it moves a little every time you sit down. Its shape and how it moves under your weight explain a large part of why some people develop tailbone pain.

Key points

  • In 1983, Postacchini and Massobrio described four coccyx shapes. The shapes that curve or angle further forward, and a slipped joint, are more common in people with tailbone pain.
  • When you sit, a healthy coccyx bends forward by about 5–25°.
  • Too much bending (over 25°), almost no movement (under 5°), or a slipped joint are each linked to pain.
  • A small bony spur at the tip is found in about 14% of patients and can press painfully into the skin when you sit.

The four coccyx shapes

Coccyx shapes (Postacchini and Massobrio, 1983).
TypeWhat it looks like from the sideWhat it means
Type IGentle forward curve, tip pointing downThe most common shape: about two-thirds of people without pain have it, but fewer than a third of people with unexplained coccyx pain
Type IIStronger curve, tip pointing straight forwardMore common in people with pain
Type IIISharp forward angle part-way downMore common in people with pain
Type IVOne of the joints has slipped (subluxation)The most clearly abnormal; strongly linked to pain

Having a type II or III coccyx does not mean you will get pain. Many people with these shapes never do. Shape is one factor among several, alongside injury, body weight and how you sit.

How the coccyx moves when you sit

Doctors compare two side-on X-rays, one taken standing and one taken sitting. This approach was developed by the French physician Jean-Yves Maigne. Comparing the two pictures shows how much the coccyx bends or slips under your weight.

Coccyx movement categories (after Maigne; summarised by Garg and Ahuja, 2021).
Finding on sitting vs standing X-rayMovementWhat it suggests
NormalBends forward 5–25°Pain more likely comes from muscles, ligaments or nerves
HypermobileBends forward more than 25°Lax, overstretched ligaments; seen more in people with a low BMI
Immobile (hypomobile)Moves less than 5°Stiff or fused joints; pain is often from pressure on the tip
Luxation (slipped joint)The lower part slips backward (or, less often, forward) when sittingThe joint gives way under load; linked to higher BMI and recent injury

In Maigne's study of 208 patients, this method found the likely cause of pain in about 69% of cases. A recent injury, within about a month before the pain started, made instability more likely.

Why we start with your history and examination

Imaging has its place, but most of the information comes from listening and a careful examination. That means finding exactly where it hurts, which movements set it off, and how the coccyx and the surrounding muscles respond to gentle pressure. Imaging is usually only needed if the pain is severe, lasts for weeks, follows a significant injury, or doesn't behave as expected.
