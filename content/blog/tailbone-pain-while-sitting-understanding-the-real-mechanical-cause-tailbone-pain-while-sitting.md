One of the most common symptoms of coccyx disorders is [Tailbone Pain While Sitting](/treatment). Many patients notice discomfort during office work, driving, or long sitting hours, yet routine scans may appear normal. According to Dr. Rajveer Singh, tailbone pain is often related to abnormal pressure distribution, posture imbalance, pelvic floor tension, and coccyx mobility issues.

## Why Sitting Triggers Tailbone Pain

When sitting, body weight shifts toward the coccyx. If the coccyx becomes inflamed, unstable, stiff, or hypersensitive, even normal sitting pressure can become painful.

## Common Causes

- Previous fall or trauma
- Long sitting hours
- Poor posture
- Weak pelvic support muscles
- Childbirth-related strain
- Sudden weight loss
- Prolonged office work

## Why MRI Reports Can Be Normal

Many patients become frustrated when MRI reports appear normal despite ongoing pain.

This happens because:

- MRI is performed in a lying position
- Sitting pressure is not evaluated
- Mechanical dysfunction may not appear on routine scans

## How Physiotherapy Helps

Specialized coccyx physiotherapy focuses on:

- Pressure reduction
- Pelvic alignment
- Sitting posture correction
- Pelvic floor relaxation
- Muscle release techniques
- Mobility restoration
- Core stabilization

## Conclusion

[Tailbone pain while sitting](https://x.com/TailboneClinic) is often caused by mechanical and functional problems rather than major structural disease. Early assessment and specialized rehabilitation can significantly improve quality of life.

## FAQs

**1. Why does my tailbone hurt only when I sit?**  Sitting increases pressure on the coccyx, making underlying dysfunction more noticeable.

**2. Can poor posture cause tailbone pain?**  Yes. Incorrect sitting posture can increase stress on the coccyx.

**3. Is tailbone pain common among office workers?**  Yes. Long periods of sitting can contribute to coccyx pain.

**4. Why is my MRI normal despite severe pain?**  Many coccyx problems are functional and may not appear on routine imaging.

**5. What is the best treatment for sitting-related tailbone pain?**  A personalized physiotherapy and rehabilitation program is often highly effective.
