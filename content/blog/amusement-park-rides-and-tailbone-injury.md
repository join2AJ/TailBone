A day at an amusement or water park is great fun, but the sudden drops, bumps and splash-downs send jolts straight up through the seat into the base of the spine. Dr Rajveer Singh regularly sees tailbone injuries from these rides, in adults and in children as young as 13 or 14.

## Key points

- Sudden vertical drops and sideways jerks can push the coccyx **inward or outward** (a subluxation).
- Children and teenagers are affected just as badly as adults.
- **Padded or thick undergarments** and a careful sitting posture absorb some of the jolt.
- If the tailbone still hurts a few days after a ride, get it checked rather than waiting.

## Why rides hurt the tailbone

On a slide or a drop ride, you land on a hard or wet surface with your whole body weight, often while leaning back. That is exactly the position that loads the coccyx most. The force is sudden, so the muscles around the tailbone don't have time to brace.

## Ride more safely

| Do | Avoid |
|---|---|
| Wear thick or padded undergarments | Thin swimwear alone on hard slides |
| Sit upright, with your weight on your thighs and sitting bones | Leaning far back on landings |
| Choose gentler rides if you've had tailbone pain before | Repeating high-drop rides one after another |
| Check on children after rides that jolt hard | Ignoring a child's complaint that sitting hurts |
Table: Dr Rajveer Singh's tips for amusement and water park rides.

## If it hurts afterwards

- Sit on a cut-out cushion and get up often (see [sitting with tailbone pain](/blog/sitting-with-tailbone-pain.html)).
- Avoid cycling and squeezing exercises for now.
- Don't let anyone press or "set" the tailbone from outside (see [why bone setting can be dangerous](/blog/tailbone-bone-setting-risks.html)).
- If pain on sitting lasts more than a few days, book an assessment. A displaced coccyx is much easier to treat early.

See a doctor urgently if there is numbness, weakness in the legs, or problems with bladder or bowel control after a fall or ride.
