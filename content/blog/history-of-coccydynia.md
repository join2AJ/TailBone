Tailbone pain is not a new problem. It has been described for centuries, and named for more than 160 years. Knowing how ideas about it developed explains why treatment today looks at the bone, the joints *and* the muscles.

::anim history-timeline

## Key points

- Accounts of pain at the tailbone go back to the **16th century**.
- In **1859**, the Scottish obstetrician **Sir James Young Simpson** named it *coccygodynia*, from the Greek for coccyx and pain.
- In **1950 and 1963**, **George Thiele** linked many cases to spasm of the pelvic floor muscles, and treated them with massage of those muscles.
- In **1983**, **Postacchini and Massobrio** described the different shapes of the coccyx.
- In **2000**, **Maigne** and colleagues used X-rays taken sitting and standing to show how the coccyx moves, and linked body weight to the type of problem.
- In **2014**, a study found tailbone pain about three times more often in people with **ankylosing spondylitis** than in people with ordinary back pain.

## Milestones

| Year | Who | What changed |
|---|---|---|
| 1500s | Early medical writers | First written accounts of pain at the tailbone |
| 1859 | Sir James Young Simpson | Named the condition *coccygodynia* |
| 1950, 1963 | George H. Thiele | Linked tailbone pain to pelvic floor muscle spasm; treated it with massage of those muscles from inside the back passage |
| 1983 | F. Postacchini and M. Massobrio | Described four coccyx shapes from X-rays (later extended to six) |
| 1991 | C. C. Wray and colleagues | Compared manipulation, injections and surgery in 120 patients |
| 2000 | J. Y. Maigne and colleagues | Sitting and standing X-rays showed an unstable coccyx in many patients; linked higher body weight to the coccyx slipping backwards |
| 2014 | R. Deniz and colleagues | Reported tailbone pain in 38% of people with ankylosing spondylitis |
Table: Milestones in understanding coccydynia.

## What it means today

Each step added a piece of the picture:

- **The muscles matter.** Thiele's work is why hands-on treatment of the pelvic floor is still part of care today.
- **The shape and movement of the coccyx matter.** Postacchini and Maigne showed that the coccyx's shape and how it moves when you sit help explain the pain and guide treatment. Read more in [coccyx shapes and movement](/blog/coccyx-shapes-and-movement.html).
- **Other conditions can be involved.** Newer research links tailbone pain with inflammatory spine disease, pelvic floor problems and more. See [conditions linked to tailbone pain](/blog/conditions-linked-to-tailbone-pain.html).

That is why our treatment combines a careful history and examination, hands-on treatment of both the coccyx and the muscles, and advice on sitting. See [coccydynia treatment, step by step](/blog/coccydynia-treatment-ladder.html).
